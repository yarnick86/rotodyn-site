The 25–75 MW gas turbine market

Mid-range machines dominate orders by unit count. Manufacturers are heavily loaded with orders for large machines, and lead times have stretched to several years. This creates an opening in the market for independent developers, local manufacturers and service companies.

The 25–75 MW segment

About 2,190 units over 2026–2035

ROTODYN estimates demand in the segment for 2026–2035 at about 2,190 units, about 99 GW and about US$44 bn of equipment value.

The 5.8% annual growth rate is deliberately set below the market average of 6.44%, because demand for large machines is growing faster. The average price of a simple-cycle turbine-generator package in the segment is about US$450/kW; the turnkey EPC cost is two to three times higher.

Method

How many 25–75 MW units are ordered each year

In 2024 around 500 gas turbines with a combined rating of about 60 GW were ordered worldwide, the highest in 22 years. Machines above 225 MW accounted for about 80% of the ordered capacity: at an average of about 400 MW, roughly 120 units and 48 GW.

The remaining 380 or so units account for about 12 GW, an average of 31.6 MW: by unit count the market is dominated by the mid-range. Reconciling units and megawatts gives about 150 units a year in the 25–75 MW class. The models named in the table alone exceed 140 units a year.

What it means for a buyer

  • One US utility paid US$25 million just to reserve a manufacturing slot.
  • GE Vernova has about 10 GW of heavy-duty slots left across 2029 and 2030 combined.
  • The bottleneck is hot gas path castings, not assembly, and every manufacturer queues for them.

What it means for the 25–75 MW class

  • Orders in the 25–75 MW band queue behind large-frame machines for the same castings.
  • Industrial users, CHP and distributed generation cannot wait three to seven years for a unit.
  • Grids adding intermittent renewable capacity need fast, dispatchable plant in exactly this band.
  • What is scarce is a tested gas generator in this class, not the capital to industrialise it.

Sources: GE Vernova Q2 2025 and Q1 2026 results (Power Engineering); Wood Mackenzie (Power Engineering, 2026); Gas Turbine World (August 2025); GridLab (September 2025); Modern Power Systems (February 2026).
