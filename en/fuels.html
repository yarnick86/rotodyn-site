Fuel flexibility: natural gas, hydrogen, syngas

A gas turbine can run on more than natural gas. We design and adapt machines for hydrogen blends, syngas from waste and biomass, biogas and liquid fuel. Fuel flexibility is built into the design from the first sketch rather than added later.

Wobbe index

What determines a switch to another fuel

The Wobbe index characterises the heat input a burner receives at a given fuel pressure drop across the nozzle. Fuels with the same Wobbe index are interchangeable without retuning the fuel system.

The Wobbe index of hydrogen is only about 12% below that of natural gas, so a blend with 20% hydrogen by volume needs no hardware changes. Biogas and syngas fall much lower: they need a dedicated fuel system, and syngas also needs its own combustor.

Option 2

Syngas from waste and biomass

Syngas produced by gasifying refuse-derived fuel (RDF) and biomass. A dedicated variant of the machine, with natural gas kept as the start-up and back-up fuel.

Syngas is not a blend: with a Wobbe index 87% below natural gas it needs its own fuel system and combustor. Its volumetric flow is 6.6 times that of natural gas.

RDF is the combustible fraction of municipal waste (plastics, paper, textiles, wood), sorted, dried and shredded, with a net calorific value of 15–18 MJ/kg. It is already produced at scale for cement kilns in most large industrial economies.
